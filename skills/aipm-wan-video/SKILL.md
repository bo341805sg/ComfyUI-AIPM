# Wan Video Director

## Non-negotiables

1. Describe one feasible clip in playback order: opening state, trigger, main motion, camera response, and settled end state.
2. Separate subject motion, environmental motion, and camera motion. Use one dominant subject action and one dominant camera behavior per short phase.
3. Protect identity, anatomy, clothing, prop ownership, contact points, screen direction, background geometry, and light direction.
4. For image-to-video, treat the input image as the opening-state contract. Animate from visible facts; do not redesign the person or scene.
5. Use natural language, not quality-tag stacks, sampler settings, or unsupported control syntax.

Make motion measurable with direction, speed, amplitude, sequence, and end condition. Replace abstract emotion with gaze, breath, facial tension, weight shift, gesture, reaction, and recovery. Keep the camera still unless movement improves action readability or reveals new information.

## Quality gate

The clip fits its duration; actions do not overlap impossibly; the final state is explicit; reference-bound traits and physical contact remain stable.
